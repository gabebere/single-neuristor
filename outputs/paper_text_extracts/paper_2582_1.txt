Abstract. The vanadium dioxide (VO
2) thin ﬁlm, usually employed as
an infrared detector, exhibits hysteresis in its resistance-temperature
characteristic. Considering a polycrystalline VO2 thin ﬁlm as a composite
medium, containing semiconducting and metallic microcrystals, the well-
known effective-medium approximation theory is employed to relate the
volume fraction of the semiconducting microcrystals to the effective ﬁlm
resistance. A phenomenological model is ﬁrst proposed for describing
the hysteretic dependence of volume fraction on temperature. From this,
a model for hysteresis in the resistance-temperature characteristic is
then derived, and a procedure for estimating the model parameters is
outlined. The model reproduces the more important hysteretic character-
istics such as the major, minor, and nested loops, in good agreement
with the experimental characteristics.

1 Introduction
Microbolometers, operating at room temperature and em-
ploying vanadium dioxide (VO 2) thin ﬁlm as the heat-
sensing material, have emerged as a promising solution for
far-infrared imaging, providing a wide range of military,
industrial, and commercial applications. The thermophysi-
cal properties of VO
2 thin ﬁlms are characterized by a mar-
tensitic solid-state phase transformation at a temperature
around 50°C. In this region, the material transforms from
semiconducting at low temperatures to metallic at higher
temperatures.
This phase transition is associated with signiﬁcant varia-
tion of the structural, electrical, and optical material
properties.
1 Recent fabrication techniques permit the depo-
sition of vanadium dioxide ﬁlms having a temperature co-
efﬁcient of resistance @TCR5(1/R)dR/dT# of 3% K 21 in
the semiconducting region2 that increases to values as large
as 200% K 21 in the hysteretic transition region. 3 Despite
this pronounced increase of the TCR, VO 2-thin-ﬁlm mi-
crobolometers are commonly operated outside the hyster-
etic region, in the semiconducting part of the resistance-
temperature ( R-T) curve. 4
Experimental results, indicating a considerable increase
in the sensitivity, have been recently reported for a
VO2-thin-ﬁlm microbolometer operating in the hysteretic
region.3 The theoretical prediction of bolometric perfor-
mance in the transition region is quite complex, due to the
hysteresis in the R-T characteristics. Thus, the analysis,
design, and optimization of a VO 2 microbolometer operat-
ing in the hysteretic region is critically dependent on the
availability of a hysteresis model that can mathematically
describe both the major and minor loops.
The development of a hysteresis model, based on the
Preisach’s theory, for describing the behavior of a VO
2 thin
ﬁlm has been recently proposed. 5 However, this model can
describe only partially the R-T characteristic, which is
quite asymmetrical. In the present work, a hysteresis model
is proposed that describes more accurately the asymmetry
of the hysteretic R-T characteristic of a VO2 thin ﬁlm. This
model is expressed in the form of an algebraic equation and
has six parameters, which can be readily determined from
experimentally obtained major and minor R-T hysteresis
loops.

3 Thermal Hysteresis in a VO
2 Thin Film
During the thermal cycling of a VO2 thin ﬁlm, the tempera-
ture at which the phase transformation of a microcrystal
occurs is greatly affected by interfacial friction between
neighboring grains,
10 accounting for an irreversible compo-
nent in the phase transition.
In the martensitic transformations 11 observed in VO 2
thin ﬁlms, three energy components are involved: the
chemical contribution, related to the structural or crystallo-
graphic change; the elastic mechanical energy; and the dis-
sipative interfacial friction energy. The ﬁrst two are consid-
ered reversible, while friction is responsible for the
irreversible energy component. This irreversible component
is the primary cause of the thermal hysteresis, which makes
the dependence of ﬁlm resistance on temperature multival-
ued.
3.1
Hysteresis Terminology
The study of hysteresis in VO 2 thin ﬁlms requires the deﬁ-
nition of some hysteresis terminology, as follows.
The curve in the R-T ~or g-T) plane resulting from the
increase of the ﬁlm temperature from 20 to 80°C is termed
the limiting descending major curve. The curve that results
when the temperature is reduced from 80 to 20°C is termed
the limiting ascending major curve . The combination of
these two curves forms the limiting major loop , which is
the principal hysteretic feature.
If, at any point along the descending or ascending major
curve, d T/dt changes sign, the resulting trajectory in the
R-T plane will approach respectively the ascending or de-
scending major curve. This point is called reversal point,
and the trajectory traversed is called a ﬁrst-order reversal
curve. Similarly, if along the ﬁrst-order reversal curve an-
other reversal point occurs, the resulting curve is called a
second-order reversal curve, and so on. The closed trajec-
tory that results from two consecutive higher-order curves
is called a minor loop . The sequence of open trajectories
formed by a sequence of higher-order curves is called a set
of nested minor loops . When the temperature is continu-
ously cycled back and forth between two identical end val-
ues, the resulting minor loop slowly drifts towards a ﬁnal
and stable equilibrium minor loop, after a few cycles. This
phenomenon is normally observed for time-varying tem-
perature excursions of small amplitude, and is called the
accommodation process.

6 Conclusion
It has been established that it is possible to describe asym-
metric hysteresis in the R-T characteristics in
VO2-thin-ﬁlm sensors by using EMA theory. For the calcu-
lation of the effective ﬁlm resistance, the EMA requires the
knowledge of the resistance and volume fraction of semi-
conducting and metallic phases, which depend on tempera-
ture. From the measured hysteretic R-T characteristic, the
hysteretic dependence of volume fraction g on temperature
T was determined. A phenomenological model for the g-T
characteristics was proposed. This model is expressed in
the form of an algebraic equation that is computationally
simple to implement. The parameters of this model were
determined from the experimental data using a fairly simple
and robust identiﬁcation procedure. The model was vali-
dated against experimental data, with regard to major and
minor loops, nested loops, and accommodation.
This paper deals only with the static features of the R-T
characteristics of a VO
2 thin ﬁlm. The effect of incident
